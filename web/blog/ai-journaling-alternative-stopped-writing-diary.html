Stopped Journaling? Let AI Write Your Daily Log Instead

July 19, 2026 · 5 min read

I've started a journal about seven times in my life. I bought the nice notebook. I downloaded Day One. I set a reminder on my phone. I wrote for three days, missed a week, felt guilty, and gave up.

I'm not alone. Most people want a record of their life. Almost nobody maintains one.

Then I found a tool that writes the journal for me — using data my photos already contain.

Why Traditional Journaling Fails

Journaling asks you to do four things at once:

  1. Remember what happened (requires good memory)
  2. Reflect on what mattered (requires mental energy)
  3. Write it down (requires time and words)
  4. Be consistent (requires discipline)

The problem is that steps 1–3 are draining, and step 4 is hard. Most people fail at step 4 because steps 1–3 cost too much daily energy.

That Day flips the model. Instead of you writing about your day, the app writes from your day — using your photos as source material.

The 10-Second Annotation vs The 500-Word Entry

I tried Day One for a year. The longest streak I managed was 11 days. Then I'd miss one, feel bad, and quit for two months.

With That Day, I annotate photos by answering three questions: Who? Where? Doing what?

That's it. Three words per photo. I can do a day's worth of photos in under two minutes.

The app takes those three words, combines them with the date, location weather, and generates a coherent narrative. It's not fiction — every sentence is grounded in something real.

What Surprised Me

1. Three words is more honest than 500 words

When I manually journaled, I filtered. I edited. I framed myself well. But with That Day, I just answer "who was I with?" — "Mom." "Where?" — "Her kitchen." "Doing what?" — "Making pasta."

The AI writes: "You were in your mom's kitchen making pasta. The afternoon light came through the window. She told you about her trip to the farmers market."

That's more real than the polished paragraphs I used to force.

2. The audio changes everything

I didn't expect to use the audio feature. But hearing your own day narrated — in a warm, natural voice — is different from reading it. It feels like someone is telling you your own story. It makes the memory feel more significant.

3. Consistency beats quality

A mediocre journal entry every day beats a perfect journal entry once a month.

That Day has never missed a morning. My streak is at 147 days. The most I ever managed on my own was 11.

My Current System

  • Photos: Taken on my phone or imported from my camera
  • Annotation: 2 minutes per day, three words per photo
  • Delivery: 8 AM email — I read it with breakfast
  • Archive: PDFs saved to a folder, searchable by date
